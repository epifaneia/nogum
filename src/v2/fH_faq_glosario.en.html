Frequently Asked Questions

The questions that are hardest to settle at the table, each with the place where it is answered. None of them brings a new rule: they all come from the Six Ideas.

There is no need to read it in one go: it is here to come back to when somebody says “so what happens if…?”. The questions came from readers and from playtests. The words, with their definitions, are in the glossary. T179

Glossary · one idea, one name

Every idea in this rulebook has a name, and only one. They are all here, with a short definition and the place where each is explained.

Access
The level of Resources needed for an object to be within your reach, from 0 (the everyday) to 3 (what should not exist). It is the first of the three locks on equipment (5.6).
Accordion
Mastery folds upwards: three Signatures in a Discipline turn the whole Discipline into +4, and the same goes for Domains and the Sphere. It does not raise the +4: it widens it (2.4).
Adjustment
The corrector for what the tally cannot express: Limited ÷2, Complete ×1, Enhanced ×2, Unleashed ×3. Vague on purpose (5.5). C·e7
Advantage
The good aspect, twin of the flaw: a permanent trait that is not a skill. It is bought, and it also arrives when the fiction owes it to you (5.8, 7.2). C·22oct
Anchor
The range of every sheet: from −2 (Null) to +4 (Signature) (2.1). C·a1
Arcane
The fifth Sphere, the one of flow: magic, psionics, ki. If any player touches it, everyone gets 14 points to share out (2.3, 5.2). C·22oct
Band
The range of numbers that gives a fumble or a critical, narrow, normal or wide depending on the card, read on the number you use (1.3). It is not the split between failure, success at a cost and success, which works on the total. C·t4
Bar
Each health line on the sheet: Physical, Mental, Spiritual and whatever the genre asks for, all with the same fractal health. It comes back whole when the GM declares you are safe (6.4, 6.5). C·c6
Blank Key
An own Key that is kept unwritten and decided the first time it is used (4.4). C·l7
Borrowed Key (or momentary Key)
The one the fiction or the GM gives you, for the plot or for a good idea. It lasts a scene and is not paid for (4.4). C·l6
Ceilings
There are three: the starting maximum, the world maximum and the player maximum. They are Grid ceilings; Keys are best left without one (3.7). C·r6
Cheap combo
Abusing the rules. The table stops it, and can say “that doesn’t count” (0.4). C·m1
Clumsy
The −1 in the other Domain of a Sphere where you do have something (2.2). C·a4
Consequence
The crack a blow leaves when it fills a block. It is not on the bar but on the sheet, and it stays until something truly heals it (6.6). C·c6
Dice
Two d10s, one red and one blue. The card says how to read them: the red alone, the sum, the best or the worst (1.3). C·t1
Difficulty
A modifier on the roll: +2 easy, 0 standard, −2 hard, −4 heroic. The TN does not move (1.3, 2.5). C·t3
Discipline
The third step of the pyramid: +2, for one point, from a closed list of forty. It is what a Key covers (2.3, 4.1). C·l3
Domain
The second step, inside a Sphere: +1, for one point. Each Sphere has two (2.2).
Engine Room
The appendix of the numbers: why each card behaves the way it does (Appendix A).
Exchange rate
A Sphere’s Grid is worth 5·n² Keys; covering it Discipline by Discipline, 8·n² (5.3). C·e2
Flaw
The disadvantage: a permanent trait that is not a skill. It gives points for something else, and it also just happens, like the scar of a filled block (5.8, 6.5). C·22oct
Fractal health
3 blocks, 9 boxes or 27 points, on the same sheet: Lethal, Tactical or War. The tone sets the zoom (6.4). C·c5
Full sheet
The whole sheet —pyramid, Grid, Keys and Style— for the protagonists and, perhaps, the main antagonist (7.3). C·c7
Fumble and critical
The extremes of the roll, read on the number you use before adding any bonus (1.3). In combat, the attacker’s critical adds +1 to damage, and so does the defender’s fumble; if both happen, +2 (6.2). C·t4
Gift
The Arcane Domain of inner power (2.3). The good aspect, twin of the flaw, is the advantage. C·22oct
GM’s gift
What the story puts in your hands as part of the premise. It is not paid for while the story asks for it (5.7). C·e6
Grid
The scale of power: one level per Sphere, from 1 (a person) to 5 (a country), and 6, the cosmic. With one level between you, the one below loses permission to try, unless they have a Key (3.1, 3.4). In Spanish, Rejilla. C·r1 C·r4
[Grid] (tag)
The equipment tag that says at what level an object operates: Standard, Level N or Ignores everything. If it puts you above your level, it is a Key (6.3). C·22oct
Guard
The attitude you go into the action with —Violence, Trick or Speciality—, declared before rolling initiative (6.1). C·c4
Key
The narrative explanation that, across a whole Discipline and against any target, puts you one or more Grid levels above your own. It cancels what it says it cancels (4.1, 4.2). C·l1 C·l2
Limited Key
The one on a leash: against a single thing, only for damage or with conditions. It costs half, rounded up, with a minimum of 1 (4.3). C·l4
Locks (on equipment)
What it takes for an object to end up on your sheet: Access, that the story allows it, and its cost in both purses (5.6). C·e5
Multiverse Bazaar
The catalogue of weapons, protection, powers and vehicles, each with its stat block and its price (9.3).
Narrative reading
One of the two ways of reading the Grid: the one above is what the story is about, and that says nothing about the world (3.2). C·r2
Null
The −2 in a Sphere where you have not spent a single point (2.2). C·a4
Out of the fight
The fourth health state: you cannot take part in the fight in any way. It is not death, which is agreed (6.4, 0.7). C·m3
Own Key
The one you buy: it is yours and it is permanent (4.4). C·l6
Permit
What no skill can do, such as flying or seeing through walls. It is paid for with Keys (4.6, 5.4). C·l9
Purse
Each of the two experience bags: points (craft) and Keys (size). They grow at different rates and do not mix (5.1). C·e3
Pyramid
Sphere (not bought) → Domain +1 → Discipline +2 → Signature +4. You use the highest step that applies (2.2). C·a3
Quadrant
The tone map: the row says which side the universe is on; the column, at what price. Seven cards and two gaps, on purpose (1.2). C·t2
Quick sheet
The one for the NPC who can hurt you but deserves no more: their Grid level and one Sphere at +2, with +0 outside it (7.4). C·c7
Reaper
What sits above the damage of any weapon: a Key to damage at its Grid level. If it reaches you and you are below, you are out of the fight, with no right to roll (4.5). C·l8
Red lines
What someone at the table does not want to live through in the game. One person is enough (0.1). C·m2
Rules Chest
The optional rules you switch on for a whole campaign (9.1). It is not the Bazaar.
Scene
The game’s unit of time: as long as a situation with its own tension lasts. What lasts one scene is not paid for (5.4). C·e7
Scenery
What has a function but no sheet and no roll. The GM declares it, and it acts as one level below the players (7.5). C·c7 C·22oct
Separate pot
What each character gets on top of their points: +1 Grid level in one Sphere, or 5 Keys. It is not traded for points (5.2). C·e1 C·r5
Session zero
The conversation before the campaign: the red lines, what is going to be felt and how far the tone goes. The numbers come out of it (chapter 0). C·m2
Signature
The fourth step: the specific skill that defines you, at +4, for two points (2.4). C·a3
Single use
What is spent by using it is not paid for. If it never runs out, it is permanent; if it has to be replenished, Limited (5.4). C·22oct
Skill points
Twelve per character, or fourteen for everyone if any player takes Arcane. Each point gives +1 on the pyramid (5.2). C·a5
Sphere
Each of the five grounds of the sheet: Physical, Mental, Social, Technical and Arcane. It is the base of the pyramid, it is not bought and gives no bonus, and in each one you have a Grid level (2.2, 3.4).
Square ladder
Raising n levels with Keys costs n² Keys (4.3). C·l4
Starting experience
The extra points and Keys, or the fewer, that the table hands out before play (5.2). C·e4
Style
The flat +2 on every full sheet, outside the pyramid. You roll it when you can no longer fail, to see how (3.5). C·a6
Success at a cost
You get it and the GM picks the price. It is a total equal to the TN or one point above, only on the cards that carry it: Unleashed Pulp and Black Comedy (1.3, 6.6). C·t5
Synchronisation
Whoever pilots a mecha, wears an exoskeleton or fuses plays with the mecha’s sheet, at its level, for as long as it lasts. It is Grid, and it is paid for as Grid (3.8). C·r9
Tags (Damage, Dodge, Soak)
The three that describe every weapon. They cancel what they say they cancel, and they override the tie (6.3). C·c3
Tangible reading
The other one: the one above really can do a hundred times more (3.2). C·r3
TN
The target number: 6, or 11 with the sum. It does not move (1.3); Tragedy is the declared exception (1.4). C·t3
Tone card
Each of the seven rule configurations on the Quadrant. It says how the dice are read, the TN, the bands and whether there is success at a cost (1.4). C·t2

If you ever come across a plain “mesh”, it is the Grid by its homely old name. Unless it belongs to a mail shirt.
