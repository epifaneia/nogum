Chapter 7 · Sheets

A sheet is the same thing for everyone: what they know, what they are, who they can touch and what it has cost them. From one sheet to another, what changes is not power, but how much of that gets written down. C·c7

In film, the lead is not lit the same way as the waiter walking past in the background. In NOGUM, the hero’s sheet should not take as long to make as the sheet for the guard who goes down in minute five. To keep the pace up, the system has four resolutions of sheet, and this chapter goes from the most central to the most disposable.

7.1 Four sheets

What changes between these sheets is bookkeeping time and dramatic detail. All four read the same rules: the roll from chapter 1, the Anchor from −2 to +4 (2.1), the Grid (3.1) and Keys (4.1). A shorter sheet is not a different rule: it is the same sheet with less written on it, and whatever is not written down is simplified.

To choose which one each character gets, a single question is enough: how long are they going to be on camera? The protagonist lives in the frame, the nemesis is there at the end, the rival comes and goes, and the scenery is the background.

7.2 The protagonist sheet, step by step

The protagonist sheet is built in the book’s order: first what they know (chapter 2), then what they are (chapter 3), next who they can touch (chapter 4) and, finally, what it has cost them (chapter 5). Each step applies a chapter, and none of them is explained again here: it is linked.

The sheet has no attributes: the broad fields of the pyramid play that role, everything goes straight into bonuses, and to shine you have to specialise (2.2). T47–T48

The six steps

  1. What the table sets. Before the sheet, session zero has already decided almost everything around it: the tone card (1.4), health and its bars (6.4, 6.5), the Grid ceilings (the starting maximum, the world maximum and the player maximum, 3.7), whether anyone at the table plays Arcane, the pace of the two purses and whether there is starting experience (5.2). If you play a recipe, almost all of it comes written in it (9.4).
  2. The concept. One sentence. Everything else is decided by looking at it.
  3. What you know (chapter 2). Spread the skill points, 12 or 14 (5.2), across the pyramid (2.2) and the closed list (2.3). If you want a +4, it is a Signature (2.4). Also note where you are left Clumsy and where Null (2.2): what you leave empty is part of the sheet too.
  4. What you are (chapter 3). The starting Grid is 1 in everything, the realistic human (3.4), except for the 2 the pot may put in (step 5). Style, +2, comes written on every protagonist’s sheet, and on full sheets (3.5).
  5. Who you can touch (chapter 4). The separate pot: one Grid level where the world allows it, or five Keys (5.2). Each Key is a whole Discipline, against anyone (4.1), and raises as many levels as you pay for (4.3); it can be left blank and decided the first time it is used (4.4). If you have Arcane, spells are paid for from here (4.7).
  6. What it costs (chapter 5). Go over the tally: the points, spent on the pyramid; the Keys or the level, in their pot; and no exchange from one purse to the other (5.1). Equipment that matters is noted down with its price (5.6, 5.4). Lastly, the aspects: advantages, flaws and the [Slow] tag (below).

Aspects: advantages, flaws and [Slow]

Positive and negative aspects are advantages and flaws, the traits that are not skills (5.8). They are bought, and they happen: the ones that are bought are noted down here, if session zero has allowed it; the rest will come in play. T87 ED-35 C·22oct

This is also where the [Slow] tag goes, for skills that are not quick in combat in your universe (5.8, 6.1). T87

Inés, from scratch

The example sheet is Inés’s, the vampire from recipe 13 (gothic horror). Her finished case file, portrait and all, is in the case files; here we tell how it was made, step by step. T187 T188

Inés, the first nights

1 · What the table sets. Recipe 13 can ask for two games, and session zero picks the first nights: the guilt of the first kiss, the fear of the first violence, the surprise of the first bullet that does not kill you. The numbers come from there. Anguish card. Health of 9 boxes. The Mental and Spiritual bars change their names: Thirst, because the craving is played like withdrawal, and Humanity, because there is someone in there who is not her. The recipe allows starting with a 2 in Physical. And since someone at the table plays Arcane (she does), everyone spreads 14 points, not 12. C·22oct Default paces, and no starting experience.

2 · The concept. “She has been dead for three nights. Her pulse still trembles when she is hungry.”

3 · What she knows. Fourteen points. You start with what defines her most, and that gives her Signature: in these nights her best weapon is not strength, it is the open door. “Being invited in” hangs from Seduction, so the whole branch is four points: Influence +1, Seduction +2 and the Signature +4, which costs two. With the Domain already paid for, Deception +2 costs her one. Then comes the rest of the concept: hunting unseen and not letting go of what she has caught.

The same Inés, at court

If session zero picks the other game, the one of court intrigue, where feeding is a formality now and the hard part is surviving the Prince’s next reception, the table changes the card to Realist and the sheet changes in a single step: the fifth. The pot goes to the Grid, and out comes Physical 2, which the recipe allows. The same fourteen points, the same Signature, the same null Technical. She has not grown.

Physical 2 does not say she is stronger. It says this story is no longer interested in the ordinary mortal: no human rolls against her in anything physical. It is the narrative reading of the Grid (3.2). Except the hunter: whoever has trained for this carries a Key, and with it they roll. And above her is the Prince, who is an NPC and stands higher than she does: without a Key, Inés cannot touch him (4.2).

For the same pot, the five Keys covered five Physical Disciplines, and the level covers all eight (5.3). The Grid comes out wider; the Keys are the ones you choose. Neither sheet is the right one: they are the two games the recipe can ask for.

7.3 Full sheets

An NPC’s full sheet is the protagonist’s, made by the GM with the same rules and the same steps (7.2). Recommendation: the GM should have only one full sheet at the table, the one for the main antagonist. Treating the villain under the same rules as the heroes (Signatures at +4, Style, the same fractal health) is what turns the final fight into a memorable event rather than a piñata of hit points.

The antagonist spends Keys like everybody else: if you want them to survive the scene, give them Keys and let it show in the fiction. Decisión del autor If they also stand above the protagonists on the Grid, they cannot be touched without a Key, like Inés’s Prince (4.2).

For everything else, simplify. NOGUM is designed so that the camera focuses on the protagonists; the rest of the world can be slightly out of focus.

7.4 Quick sheets

In the app: the fifth form, The cast, builds these two sheets, the quick one and the scenery one, in three taps, and tells you along the way whether Style is rolled against that NPC (3.5), whether there is a real fight or whether there is nothing to roll. Not the nemesis: that one is made on the sheet, with the same rules as a player character.

Using a full sheet for an NPC who is not crucial is not recommended. To improvise, use the quick sheet, which fits on an index card and takes ten seconds to make by answering two questions:

  1. What level is it? (Grid 1–5). This decides whether the protagonists roll Style against it or have to fight for real. Careful: the number says nothing on its own. You read it next to yours. A Grid 2 can be the chrome psycho who wrecks you tonight or, three arcs later, the same monster you no longer pay attention to. Do not raise its Grid when you rise (3.6): leave it where it is and let it fall behind. That is half the point.
  2. Which Sphere is it good in? One whole Sphere of the five, not a single skill: Physical, Mental, Social, Technical or Arcane. Golden rule: inside that Sphere it rolls at +2; outside it, at +0. A whole Sphere is a lot, and that is on purpose: a quick NPC does not defend itself with nuance, it defends itself with one clear idea: the muscle, the one who knows books, the one who talks you round, the one who fixes machines, the one who does magic. If you need it good in two, that is already a full sheet. The +0 outside is a GM simplification for quick sheets: it saves the sums. An NPC with a full sheet follows the Clumsy and Null rules like anyone else (2.2). T176 C·22oct

Health: use fractal health (6.4), but do not pick its health according to its “real” strength. Ask yourself how long you want the fight to last. By default, three boxes: healthy, wounded and out.

7.5 Scenery sheets

Scenery is what is no longer interesting: you cannot fail against it (0.6), so it has no sheet and no roll. Since it does not roll, it acts as n−1, with n the players’ Grid: one level below them, with no need to write it down. It does not even need a Grid level. C·c7 C·22oct The GM declares what is scenery, and it is settled with Style, like everything below your level: you roll to see how, not whether (3.5). T176

Whatever sits far above you is not scenery: it is higher up than you are. Without a Key you do not roll against it: you go around it, you run, or you look for a Key; and if it comes for you, the defeat is narrated (3.1). Up there is usually where the cosmic stays (level 6, 3.7), which in most worlds is unplayable. T183 C·22oct

7.6 Clarifications

Can scenery hurt me? No: you can no longer fail against it, and you roll Style to see how. Whatever hurts you without you being able to roll is not scenery: it is above you, and without a Key the defeat is narrated (7.5). C·22oct

When does an NPC move from a quick sheet to a full one? When you need it to be good in two spheres, or when it is the nemesis. The protagonists having risen does not move it up: that one gets left behind (7.4).
