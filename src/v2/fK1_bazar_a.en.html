9.3 The Multiverse Bazaar

The Bazaar is the tally from Chapter 5 done hundreds of times over. Every item carries its stat block and its price in Access, points and Keys, so you can see what something like yours costs and extrapolate. C·e5

This is reference material, not study material. It is NOGUM’s gear porn: a shop window of weapons, armour, powers and gadgets from ten settings, plus a social arsenal. Nobody has to memorise it. Open it when you need a weapon for an NPC, when a player asks “so what does this do?” or when you want to hear how your genre sounds in numbers.

The stat blocks are teaching crutches: they convey tone better than theory does. An Energy Sword carrying Soak: None and a Plasma Rifle carrying Soak: Max 1 explain space opera faster than three pages of essay. A weapon is not defined by its price, but by how it bends the rules: can it be dodged? Is armour any use against it? Does it reach someone higher up the Grid? And the price comes straight out of that.

There are no registered trademarks in the catalogue. The names you see are descriptive on purpose —the blade nobody can lift, the cloud of the pure of heart, the slow-time implant— and the proof that they work is that you know exactly which object I mean.

How to read a stat block

Each item states with explicit tags what it does with damage, dodging, soak and the Grid, and in three columns what it costs. The combat tags are explained in 6.3; here is just enough to read the table.

Damage 0–2
Points the blow crosses off if it lands clean: 1 = light (“A&E”), 2 = serious (“ICU”). If the attacker rolls a critical, +1 to damage. A weapon’s damage goes up to 2; anything beyond that is not more damage, it is The Reaper with its level. Specials: Mental Damage N and Social Damage N (social damage goes to the Mental bar unless the campaign creates a bar of its own). C·c2
The Reaper · Grid N
It goes in the damage column. Anyone below N who takes the blow is out of the fight, with no right to roll (4.5). It is a Key, and it cancels what it says it cancels: whatever its dodge tag leaves open can be attempted (4.2). Against someone already at level N, it is an ordinary blow. C·l8 C·22oct
Dodge
What the dodge reaction is allowed to do. Full: the reduction can bring damage down to 0. Partial (area, bursts, fire): even if you mitigate, you take at least 1 point. None: it cannot be dodged. Hard (−2) and Easy (+2) modify the dodge roll (ED-10). A pistol or an ordinary firearm cannot be dodged at level 1; space-opera blasters can, by genre convention. C·e8 C·22oct
Soak
What armour is allowed to do. Full: it can cancel all the damage. Max N: it reduces at most N points. None: armour is useless. Specials: Mind, Will and Reputation, resisted with the mental or social bar or skill.
Grid
Relationship with the Grid. Standard: it does not reach higher levels; this is the default when the stat block says nothing. Level N: the item operates at that level (vehicles, power armour). If it is a mecha, an exoskeleton or power armour, whoever wears it plays with its sheet and its level: Synchronisation (3.8). An ordinary vehicle passes on nothing. T181 Key: creature: a Key against one kind of being. Any level: up to the ceiling of your universe; only the impossible blade.
Access · Points · Keys
What it costs you to have it, in the two currencies plus the world’s permit (5.6). Access is the level of Resources (Social → Hierarchy → Resources) you need for that object to be within your reach: 0 everyday, 1 serious weapon, 2 military hardware, 3 what should not exist. Points, skill points, for what the object makes you better at. Keys, from your purse, for what it lets you do that nobody else can. Prices are worked out for a level 1 bearer. T176 T183 The catalogue’s Access values come from damage and rarity: the GM can move any of them one step without breaking anything.

Where the numbers in the columns come from

It is the tally from 5.4 applied to the tags. When it is not obvious, each item’s note spells it out.

  • Damage is not paid for. It is the only free thing about a weapon. C·e8
  • Whatever makes defence harder, in points. Dodge: Partial, 1; Dodge: Hard (−2), 2; Soak: Max 1, 1. With Damage 2, Soak: Max 2 only takes something off on a critical, and is not charged; with Damage 1, neither is Soak: Max 1.
  • Every bonus, in points. +1 to a roll, 1 point; soaking N points, N. Best of 2d10 counts as +2, and Worst of 2d10 for whoever attacks you, as −2. C·e5
  • Whatever it cancels, in Keys. One Key per trait. Dodge: None or Soak: None, even in a single case, is a one-level Key: 1. In a weapon without the Reaper, both at once are two things it cancels, and each pays its own: 2. The Key cancels what it says it cancels, and whatever it does not say is still rolled (4.2). C·e8 C·22oct
  • The Reaper and the Grid, in Keys. The Reaper of level N lifts you n = N − 1 levels above your Grid, and it covers damage, not hitting, so it is Limited: half of n², rounded up, and at one level it costs the same as the Complete one, because the minimum is 1 (Grid 2, 1; 3, 2; 4, 5; 5, 8; 6, 13) (5.6). Its Soak: None is included and does not pay the extra Key: massive damage wrecks the shield too, and the same concept is not charged twice. The same goes for a level Key Limited to damage, which is the Reaper under another name (Super Strength, the Worthy Hammer). Not being dodgeable is another trait: if it also cannot be dodged, 1 more Key; if its dodge is hard or partial, or its soak is capped, that makes defence harder and goes in points, as with any weapon. A vehicle with a level pays its level’s Key in full, n², with its Reaper included. Inheriting the level of a whole Sphere (Synchronisation) is Grid: 5·n² (5.3). C·l4
  • Every permit, in Keys. Flying, seeing through things, doing damage without a weapon: 1 Key if it is permanent, even if the Access is 0. A spell is a permit (4.7). If a power is a permit and also cancels, it pays for both. C·l9 C·l10
  • Single-use things are not paid for. Grenades, potions, scrolls, drugs. If it never runs out, it is permanent; if it has to be restocked and you can run out of it, Limited (5.4). C·22oct
  • The object’s drawbacks are no discount. Dodge: Easy (+2) or the −2 to Stealth of a set of plate armour are what the object is. They can make it Limited, and anything permanent costs at least 1. C·e7

The Adjustment (5.5) is done by rule of thumb. If your table sees it differently, change it: the system is vague on purpose.

The power tables carry no Access: powers describe what you already are, and they are paid for in points and Keys (5.6). T176

Procedure reminder (6.2): the attacker rolls against the TN of the active card. If the attack connects, the defender makes one reaction (dodge or soak, whichever the weapon allows) and the two result levels are compared: if the defender matches or beats the attacker, there is no damage; if they succeed but stay below, −1; if they fail, full damage. All of this within the limits of the tag. The “Notes” column also holds usage tags: Permit (makes the impossible possible), Fractal (heals or affects a bar), Effect, Immunity and Key: creature (a narrative Key against one kind of being). A “—” means the entry does not apply.

Editorial decisions in this appendix
  • ED-B1 The tags dropped in T68 (Type A/B/C, Dirty/Lethal/Absolute, [FAST], [LETHAL], [AREA], [INEVITABLE]) are converted to the explicit format: Type A → Dodge: Full Soak: Full; Type B → Soak: Max 1; Type C / “Inevitable” → Dodge: None Soak: None. “Turn Dirty damage into Aggravated” becomes “the damage becomes Soak: None”.
  • ED-B2 “[+1 Die]” does not exist in the canon: NOGUM does not add dice. It is converted to “the roll uses Best of 2d10 (High Optimism)” for whatever action or period the item specifies. The other [Engine] tags are converted the same way: “Engine: Best of 2d10” is that same reading; “Engine: High Chaos” means using the Black Comedy card; “Engine: Extra Action” is rewritten as an effect (one more action per turn).
  • ED-B3 Items take people out of the fight; death is agreed (0.7). Where the source said “if you die”, “kills” or “dies”, read “if the character leaves the story” or “is out of the fight”.
  • ED-B4 Prices redone on 22 October on the author’s word: the Access · Points · Keys columns follow the tally in 5.4; Damage 3 and Massive, Absolute and Infinite damage become the Reaper with its level; ordinary firearms cannot be dodged at level 1 and pay a Key; single-use things are not paid for.
  • Duplicates and variants: the most recent approved version is kept, and the other is noted under “Notes”. Merged items only count once, so some settings drop below 30.
  • Values outside the legend ([Dodge: Surprise], [Dodge: Full (Slow)], [Soak: None (Metal)], [Soak: None in gaps], [Damage: Fall], [Return Damage], [Key: creature]…) are kept as they are, and the “Notes” column explains how to read them.
  • The idea bank from the first catalogue (T67) is not included, because its tag format was replaced in T68.

The workshop: what is not on the list

No list of objects is ever enough, least of all in a universal rulebook. The catalogue is a table of equivalences: if yours is not there, look for the closest thing and it costs the same; if it is half of one, round up; if it resembles nothing, do the tally from 5.4, with its Adjustment (5.5), and its Access by the Resources drawers (5.6).
