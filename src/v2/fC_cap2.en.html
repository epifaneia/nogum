Chapter 2 · What You Know (the Anchor)

Every sheet runs from −2 to +4. That is why the statistics work exactly as they were designed to: in any genre, with any tone card and in session one hundred. That range is the Anchor. C·a1

What you know how to do, your trade, is measured in that range and in that range alone. How big the things you do are is another question, and it has chapters of its own: what you are (the Grid, chapter 3) and who you can touch (the Key, chapter 4).

2.1 The Anchor: from −2 to +4

Let’s start with a statistical truth: being a master means failing little. In most roleplaying games, levelling up means inflating your numbers to the point of absurdity. In NOGUM, competence is a closed value. We define mastery not by how much damage you deal, but by the reliability with which you carry out your will. And reliability fits in seven numbers.

Those numbers come from a four-step pyramid, explained in 2.2: the Sphere, a whole field, such as the physical or the social; the Domain, one of its two halves; the Discipline, a trade within the Domain; and the Signature, the top of that trade.

There is no row for +3: it does not come off the sheet. If bonuses reach +3 once they are applied (2.5), that +3 has no name: it may be a professional with help, or a clumsy one with a great deal of help. C·22oct

And here is the crux of the system: the range does not change, whatever genre you are playing. The rubber pirate is a Grand Master (+4) in Brawling. The hitman who avenges dogs is a Grand Master (+4) in Brawling. A neighbourhood boxer in a realist film is a Grand Master (+4) in Brawling. They all have the same modifier, roll the same two dice and have the same chance of hitting their opponent with the same card (in Epic, almost certain). The statistics are identical because their degree of mastery is identical: they are the best at what they do. C·a1

But if they all have the same number… why does the rubber pirate’s punch shatter an island, while the punch of the hitman who avenges dogs only shatters a jaw? Because the bonus does not say that. The Grid says it (3.1), and sometimes a Key (4.1). The bonus says whether you do it well; the Grid, whether your perfect success “opens a door” or “opens a dimensional portal”. T26

That’s why NOGUM doesn’t add dice as power goes up. In games where you roll fistfuls of dice, the fumble probability breaks. Here we anchor the statistics, and what moves is the world. T26

What comes out of the range

This is how NOGUM manages to be universal without being generic. Several things fall out of the closed range on their own:

  • The bonus tells you that you are competent, that you don’t make silly mistakes and that your hands are steady. It keeps the character internally consistent from one game to the next.
  • The emotion of the dice stays anchored to the tone of the game, or of the scene. What makes a roll harsh or kind is the card (1.3), not a sheet that has got out of hand. That is why changing card really does change the game, and a +4 in Anguish is still a +4.
  • The tables can be calculated in full: seven bonuses per card, and that’s it. You have them all in Appendix B, and the reasons in the Engine Room (A.6).
  • Experience makes nobody taller, only wider. Once you have your +4, points make you good at more things, not a god at one (2.4, 5.8). In session one hundred, the veteran rolls the same as on day one when doing their own thing; what has changed is everything else they know how to do.
  • No roll becomes trivial because of the numbers: at +4 you can still fail (0.6). C·a2

2.2 The pyramid (the four zoom levels)

The range is shared out by a pyramid. Apart from Style, a separate +2 rolled when you can no longer fail (3.5), it is the only way to have a skill bonus on the sheet, and it ends at +4: that is why no sheet leaves the range. C·a3

At NOGUM we love finding unexpected ways out. The rulebooks we have used as references pick a side: either very generic, narrative skills, or very precise skills that turn choosing them into an ordeal. We want, or at least that is the intention, to keep the best of both worlds. That is why the pyramid has four levels of “zoom”, from the most open to the most specific. NOGUM’s thesis is that a general skill covers the basics: knowing what the thing is about. Take History. Someone who knows history in general probably knows the date of American independence, but to master the details of Korea’s Three Kingdoms period (and, within it, the rise and fall of the Gaya confederacy) we are talking about someone specialised: in that place, in that period, or in both. In the rules, this means the bonus of a general skill is capped. To shine, you must specialise: you buy the general skill as a base so you can specialise in the next step up.

There are no attributes

In NOGUM there is no Strength, Reflexes or Intelligence. The broad fields of the pyramid are big enough to replace them. T47–T48 Everything on the sheet goes straight to bonuses, from −2 to +4.

Think of a satellite map: the Sphere is the continent, the Domain the city, the Discipline the street and the Signature the house number. The closer you zoom in, the more power you have and the narrower your view.

Everything costs the same: one point gives exactly +1 T101–T103. The greater breadth of the lower levels is elegantly balanced by their bonus cap. You only become a Grand Master in very specific things.

  • Two maxed-out Signatures sharing a Domain and Discipline: 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 6.

Null and the cascading safety net

The Null and Clumsy Rule

If you have not spent a single point in a Sphere, you are Null in it: you do not roll at +0, you roll at −2.

As soon as you buy anything within that Sphere, Null disappears —but not entirely—. Buying anything means buying at least one Domain, so your column, the Domain you have touched, works normally: you roll with whatever you have in it, +1 at the very least. Whatever falls in the Sphere’s other column you roll at −1: you are clumsy. T106 T131 T170 C·a4

Clumsy is not Null. It is better than Null: the Sphere is not foreign to you, you know about nearby things and can at least tell what would be a silly move. But it is worse than having the column, and for a specific reason: you have the Sphere, but the wrong half of it. You are pure Vigour, strength and stamina, and you are asked to tumble. You are pure Erudition, the facts in books, and you are asked to follow a trail across the hills (the full list of Domains is in 2.3). You are not short of muscle or of brains: you are short of footing.

And that is why the sheet matters. Five Spheres of two Domains each make ten columns (eight if there is nothing supernatural in your world, the Arcane Sphere of 2.3), and covering them all eats almost the whole sheet. With the starting budget (5.2) you are going to be clumsy at something, and that is the decision. A broad character is almost never clumsy and almost never shines; a deep one shines at their own thing and drags everywhere else. Both sheets are legal and the game rewards neither: it rewards the table knowing which one each player made, and splitting the work accordingly.

For each action you use the highest applicable step: the steps of the pyramid do not add up. A Signature is not +4 on top of its Discipline’s +2: it is the +4. C·a3 Example: defusing a nuclear bomb.

  1. Do you have the Explosives Signature? You roll at +4.
  2. If not, do you have the Security Discipline? You roll at +2.
  3. If not, do you have the Operations Domain? You roll at +1.
  4. If not, have you bought anything in the Technical Sphere (the other Domain, Artifice)? You roll at −1: you are clumsy.
  5. If your Technical Sphere is empty, you roll at −2: you will probably cut the red wire because it is prettier.

If a broad skill on your sheet is close enough to what you are attempting, you negotiate it with the GM on an equal footing. T82

2.3 The closed list (your character’s skeleton)

For the range to mean the same at every table, the steps have to be the same at every table. That is why the first three are a closed list.

Before it overwhelms you: a list of forty Disciplines is daunting on a page, and rightly so. We have kept it because the app narrows it down for you: it shows you only what your world allows. If you enjoy reading long catalogues the old-fashioned way, you have them complete in the toolbox of chapter 9 (9.2) and in its catalogue, the Bazaar (9.3), for pleasure, not out of obligation.

NOGUM wants to make things easy for you and not share a fate with some lazy rulebooks out there. That is why we give a closed, universal list for the first three levels: it is NOGUM’s skeleton. The Signature, which is much broader, we invite you to create yourself (2.4). We believe this structure helps players sketch their characters first in broad strokes and, when it is time for detail, know exactly where to put the pencil. Borrador

Knowing is not doing

Academic knowledge is Mental: theoretical biology lives in Erudition → Sciences. Actually healing someone is Technical: Artifice → Biotech. T102

2.4 Signatures

The Signature is the top of the pyramid and the ceiling of the range: +4, for two points. It is the only open step, because you write the top of each trade yourself. C·a3

  • They are broad, like the skills of other games: Firearms rather than Sniper; Swordmaster rather than a specific model of sword. T49, T98, T100
  • They hang logically from a Discipline. Examples: Weapons → Historical Fencing; Systems → Cryptography; Biotech → Neurosurgery; Sciences → Chemistry; Acrobatics → Parkour (which works for crossing the city over the rooftops, not for walking the circus tightrope).
  • Golden rule: to be a Grand Master (+4) in something, you must be able to explain exactly what you are doing.
  • A catalogue of two hundred Signatures in section 9.2, ordered by Discipline: five for each, which is how they get looked up at the table.
  • The Signature gives bonus, not size. Size comes from the Grid and the Keys, and a Key covers a whole Discipline (4.1).

The accordion: mastery folds upwards

If you are a Grand Master with the pistol, the rifle and the sword, you are no longer a specialist in three things: you are a Grand Master of Weapons. And if you are one in three Disciplines of Vigour, you are a Grand Master of the whole of Vigour. Mastery does not pile up: it folds. ED-38

When you have…It folds intoAnd then you roll
3 Grand Master Signatures in the same DisciplineThe whole Discipline+4 at anything in that Discipline, Signature or not

Both folded DomainsThe whole Sphere+4 at anything falling in that Sphere

It is the other way to grow, and the only one left to points once you have your +4: the range still does not move —no sheet goes past +4— but the +4 keeps widening until it covers everything. A character who has folded the Physical Sphere is no stronger than one with a single Signature: it is just that there is no longer anything physical they cannot do.

And it is genuinely expensive, which is what makes it a campaign goal rather than a creation trick:

Folding…PointsHow much that is
One Discipline8More than half a fresh sheet

One Sphere44Forty-four sessions at one point per session (5.8)

With the recommended starting points (5.2), the most that fits is one folded Discipline and little else, so nobody arrives at the table as the best in the world at a complete field. Folding a Sphere is, literally, the end of a long campaign: that is where points go when there is nothing left to learn.

If three feels like too many or too few at your table, it is the only number this accordion has: raise it to four to make total mastery nearly unreachable, or drop it to two for campaigns of competent heroes from the start.

2.5 Bonuses stack

In the roll, your skill (the highest applicable step) is added to everything that weighs on it: difficulty, equipment, effects. Whatever can stack, stacks, at the table’s discretion, and anything above +4 is rolled at +4 (below). C·a2 C·22oct

The roll formula is in 1.3; combat adds rules of its own (chapter 6).

Difficulty (the complexity filter)

Difficulty does not depend on the character’s skill, but on the intrinsic complexity of the task. The TN is set by the tone card and does not move (1.3). The task’s difficulty is expressed as a modifier on the roll: C·t3

ModifierDifficultyExamples
+2EasyRoutine tasks under stress.
0StandardThe default challenge: shooting someone who is moving, hacking a commercial server.
−2HardRequires professional competence: a headshot, open-heart surgery.
−4HeroicMathematically unlikely for an ordinary human. Requires stacked bonuses.

The success rule and the numerical limits

  • Add up all modifiers: skill, difficulty, equipment and effects. Whatever can stack, stacks, and what can is decided by the table, case by case. C·22oct
  • If the total ends up above +4, you roll at +4. The excess is margin, and you can make use of it: take penalties, attempt something harder with a bigger prize, and roll with your +4. C·a2
  • If the total ends up below −2, the GM decides whether there is a right to roll; if there is, it is at −2. C·22oct
  • The only reason not to roll is that failing is not interesting, not that failure is unlikely (0.6). C·a2 C·m4

Your Grand Master is going to hit 99% of the time in Epic? Perfect: that is what the genre asks for. We don’t inflate difficulty to compensate. But that 1% exists, and if failing would be interesting, you roll. And if the player wants to show off, let them raise the difficulty themselves: one hand behind the back, the bullet bounced off the wall. Swap safety for glory. T21–T22

What stacks is what the fiction asks for, not what you find by rummaging through the rulebook: squeezing the rules for a number is the cheap combo (0.4). C·m1 C·22oct

Fumbles, criticals and successes at a cost depend on the active tone card (1.4), and they are read on the number you use, without bonuses (1.3). That is why going past +4 does not save you from the fumble: the margin is for daring more, not for no longer taking risks.

What each bonus weighs

What the things you wrote on your sheet mean in numbers. Figures with the First Gear card (red die against TN 6):

  • Clumsy (−1): the wrong foot. You have the Sphere but that column is empty. Your chance drops from 50% to 40%: a little, and it shows. With the worst of 2d10 (Anguish card) it falls to 16%, and there it is no longer a little. It is what happens to the strongman when he is asked for a somersault.
  • Null (−2): the dead weight. With the Sphere empty you suffer −2. Your chance of success drops from 50% to 30%. With the worst of 2d10 (Anguish card) it falls to 9%: you are practically a walking corpse. Null turns routine tasks into mortal dangers.
  • Competence (+1): the citizen. Having the Domain gives you a slight nudge: from 50% to 60%. You are functional. There is no +0 on the pyramid (2.1). T170 T183
  • The discipline (+2): the professional. You are reliable. A +2 turns a TN 6 into a virtual TN 4 and drastically reduces the weight of bad luck (70%).
  • The Grand Master (+4): reliability. With +4, on a standard task you only fail on a natural 1, which is the fumble (90% success). If the tone is optimistic (Epic card), you are almost infallible at mundane things (99%). C·22oct With the Realist’s sum (TN 11), 85%: five points less than with one die, even though the scale is twice as long, because the bell curve gathers the results in the middle and four points of bonus move a lot of probability. That 15% is that card’s grain of reality. T29–T30

The seven figures for each card, with fumbles, criticals and costs, are in Appendix B.

Temporary and permanent modifiers

Besides skill and difficulty, what goes into the sum are modifiers. Equipment brings its own, and its price is covered in 5.6.

Temporary (they last a scene, a few turns or until a condition is met)
Stunned, Inspired, Drugged, Knocked Down… They come in three kinds: bonuses (numerical, they modify your roll), Keys (narrative: they let you act above your Grid level; the ones that last only a moment are in 4.4) or effects (mechanical or narrative). Define the condition under which they go away (end of scene, healing, ritual, number of turns…). Any effect that comes from a piece of equipment is temporary, because it disappears if you are not carrying it.
Permanent (they last an arc or a campaign)
These are changes to the sheet: scars, traumas, corruption, prostheses. They need no condition to be there: they simply are. They are removed or modified with experience (5.8) or, in the case of lasting injuries, with another significant arc (6.6).

2.6 Clarifications

I have the Discipline (+2) and the Signature (+4). Do I roll at +6? No: you roll at +4. The steps of the pyramid do not add up; what stacks are the roll’s modifiers (2.2).

I am a Grand Master and I carry a master gunsmith’s rifle (+1). What good is it to me? It lets you dare. On a normal task you roll at +4 anyway; but if you set yourself a hard one (−2) —the shot to the hand instead of the body, or from further away—, the rifle leaves you at +3 instead of +2 (2.5).

The GM’s character with a quick sheet, the ten-second one (7.4), rolls at +0 outside their own thing, not at −2. Is that a mistake? It is a simplification those sheets make, and it stays within the range. C·22oct

Does Style (+2) add to my skill? No. Style sits outside the pyramid and is rolled on its own, when you can no longer fail (3.5).
