Glossary · one idea, one name

This rulebook has been through a vocabulary clean-up: each idea has one name and only one. If you come from an earlier draft, the equivalences are here.

Session zero
The conversation before writing the campaign: everyone’s red lines, what is going to be felt, what solutions the genre asks for and, from that, the numbers (chapter 0).
Director’s gift
What the story puts in your hands as part of the premise —the agency’s robot, the group’s ship—. It is not paid for while the story asks for it (3.6).
Quadrant
The tone map: three rows (which side the universe is on) by three columns (how much it shows), with seven cards and two gaps. It used to be called the “Quadrant”, and the word grid named three other things in this same book.
Card
Each of the seven rule configurations on the Quadrant. There is one for the table at a time, unless you use different tones for each character (7.3).
Band
The range of natural numbers that gives a fumble or a critical. “Fumble band 1–2” means a 1 or a 2 on the number you use is a fumble. Do not confuse the bands with the split of results (failure, success at a cost, clean success), which works on the total and not on the die.
Success at a cost
You get it and the GM picks the price: time, a resource, position or friendly fire. It takes the target number and the point just above it (with TN 6, totals 6 and 7). This is the tool we borrow from the black comedy systems family, with thanks.
Sphere
Each of the five grounds of the skill pyramid: Physical, Mental, Social, Technical and Arcane. It is not bought and gives no bonus. In the draft this layer was called the “Grid”.
Domain
The second step of the pyramid, inside a Sphere. Worth +1.
Discipline
The third step, inside a Domain. Worth +2.
Signature
The fourth step: the specific skill that defines you and that everyone recognises. Worth +4. It has no steps: a Signature is the top of that Discipline or it is not a Signature.
Power Grid
The scale of power: it measures what you play with and at what scale, not how good you are. One level of difference already settles the scene. The draft called it “Grid” in some places and “Power Grid” in others.
[Grid]
The gear tag that says how an item behaves against the scale of power: Standard, Key (Level X) or Ignores everything. It used to be the [Mesh] tag.
Key
What lets you affect something above your Power Grid level: a weapon, a weakness, some help, a true name. With a Key, you roll as normal.
Style
The flat +2 every full sheet carries, outside the pyramid. It is used against foes below your level: you win for certain and the roll says how good you look.
Guard
The attitude you enter the action with: Violence, Trick or Specialty. It is declared before rolling initiative and marked on the sheet; if the table does not choose, the last mark applies. The draft called it “Guard”.
Fractal Health
The three blocks of health you can look at close up or far away: three boxes (Lethal), nine (Tactical) or twenty-seven (War). The proportions never change, only the zoom.
Chest
The optional rules you switch on for a whole campaign. Do not confuse it with the Multiverse Bazaar, which is the equipment catalogue.
Set dressing
Whatever has a function and a level but no sheet and no health: the crowd, the sleeping dragon, the storm.
Separate pot
What each character gets on top of their 12 or 14 points: +1 Grid level in one Sphere, or 5 Keys. It does not trade for points in either direction. A Grid level is the Sphere’s eight Disciplines for five Keys: five eighths of a Key per Discipline. The Key’s freedom costs 60 % more. T176
Own Key
The one you buy and write down, always against its target: what it has written on it. “Weapons against vampires” works against vampires, not against a werewolf. Narrow: one Discipline, for ever; never a Signature. Broad: the whole Sphere, for one scene; it is crossed out when the scene ends. T176
Lent Key (or Key of a moment)
The one the Director, or the fiction, gives you for the plot or for a good idea: the hacker club, twenty mercenaries at once. It lasts one scene and raises whatever the fiction says; it is not improved with Keys. It goes on the sheet in pencil and is rubbed out when the scene ends.
Wallet
Each of the two experience bags: skill points and Keys. They grow at different rates and do not mix. The Key one is the campaign’s power dial.
Ceiling
The highest whole Grid a player can have in a Sphere in your world. The recipe proposes it and the table confirms it at session zero. It does not stop Keys: one Discipline can go above it if your world makes it possible (2.4).
Access
The level of Resources needed for an object to be within your reach: 0 everyday, 1 serious weapon, 2 military hardware, 3 what should not exist.
Scene
This game’s unit of time. It lasts as long as a situation with its own tension: a duel, an interrogation, the whole assault on a castle. Tone, Style, the wide Key and the looted weapon are all measured in scenes.
Flaw
The scar of a filled block: what is left when a block of your Mental or Spiritual bar fills up and you later heal. It has no number, it has consequences. Its good twin is the gift, and that is not bought either.
Accordion
The rule that folds mastery upwards: three Grand Master Signatures turn the whole Discipline into +4, three Disciplines the Domain, and both Domains the Sphere. It does not raise the ceiling: it widens it. It is the long ladder of points, as the square ladder is of Keys.

The word that gave most trouble was “grid”: it came to mean four things at once —the tone map, the sphere layer, the scale of power and a gear tag. They are now Quadrant, Sphere, Power Grid and [Grid]. If a stray grid is still hiding somewhere in the book, and it is not chainmail, it is a typo.
